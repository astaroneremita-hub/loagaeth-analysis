# What the sources say the book is

For most of this study the tables were treated as an unknown text to be measured. The diaries that
record how the book was received say plainly what it is, what it is for, and how it is — and is
not — to be read. This page sets that record beside the measurements. It should have been written
first.

Sources read (November 2026):

- **Sloane MS 3188**, Dee's own diary, *Liber Mysteriorum Quintus* and its Appendix (23 March – 23 May
  1583): read from a microfilm of the manuscript. "S" below; page = page of that PDF (folio = page − 2).
- **A True and Faithful Relation** (Casaubon, 1659), the diaries from 28 May 1583: OCR text checked
  against page images for the passages quoted. "T" below; page = page of the PDF.
- *Mysteriorum Libri I–III* (1581–82) in typed transcription, for the earlier tables.
- Detailed reading notes, with every statement located, are kept in `data/raw/notes_sloane3188.md`
  and `data/raw/notes_tfr.md` (not redistributed). Spelling is the source's; a few readings in rapid
  hand are secure in sense but not letter for letter, and are marked in those notes.
- **Sloane MS 3189**, the book itself, in Kelley's hand: 76 microfilm images covering ff.3–57 (greyscale,
  about 30 pixels to a cell: ink colour cannot be judged and n/u often cannot be told apart). "M" below;
  notes in `data/raw/notes_sloane3189_images.md`. Table leaf n is folio n + 8.

## 2. How it was delivered and written

- Leaf 1, side 1: 49 rows of 49 squares; each square was dictated as a string of letter-names, then
  read as a word. From row 3 "every word shall be named". A square holds from 1 to 12 letters; a word
  may fill a square, share it, or span two. (S 71–80)
- 21 characters, counted "from the right hand toward the left", named Pa, Veh, Ged … Gisg, in three
  sevens. (S 66–67)
- Leaf 1, side 2: 40 rows of words and 9 rows of single letters; Kelley wrote it himself in Latin
  letters and "understode the Language and sense therof, as if it had byn english", then lost it.
  "The other leaves are written, apart, in an other boke" — Sloane 3189. (S 83, 88)
- By 18 April "24 leaves" (elsewhere "30 Tables") were written in 21 days; the tables were finished
  on 6 May. (S 91, 106)
- "Forme, and write the boke after thyne own Judgment." Dee's plan: begin at our end, as a Hebrew
  bible; leaf 1 "cannot be written in such little and æquall squares, as all the rest of the 47
  leaves are". (S 108–109)
- "Write the Book (after your order) backward, but alter not the form of letters … in respect of the
  places." "The first leaf … is the last of the Book"; it is "a hotchpot without order", signifying
  "a disorder of the World". (T 113)
- "Write the 49. You have but 48 already": the 21 words, "in 5 Tables, in every Table 21 Letters".
  (T 113–115)
- "In the last seven of the 40 dayes, the words of this Book shall be distinguished" and accented.
  Where the letters do not fit: "There is one superfluous." (T 117)
- From 29 June 1583 the writing is taken over ("Leave it where you will … you shall find it
  written"); in 1584: "Bind up together, 48 leaves; whose skin shall bear Silver", for "man is not
  worthy to write that shall be written". The silvering failed (16 Aug 1584). (T 121, 249–250, 307)

## 3. How it is read

- Along the top row from the right hand to the left, then the next row down. (S 66, 71, 109)
- Pronunciation is given word by word for leaf 1: accents, long vowels, the sounds of the letter
  names. (S 67, 74–77)
- **"There is no other reading of the Book, but the appearing of the Ministers and Creatures of
  God."** (Gabriel, 13 July 1584; T 299)

## 4. How it is used — and the limits set on that

- "It is not to be spoken, but in the time of his own time." (S 71)
- "When it is written, reade it no more with voyce, till it be in practise." (Raphael, S 77)
- **"Interpret not, till your understanding be furnished."** (S 80)
- **"Seke not to know the mysteries of this boke, tyll the very howre that he shall Call thee."**
  (Uriel, 18 April 1583; S 91)
- The book is finished "One thing excepted: which is the use therof"; a day is appointed
  (1 August 1583, after nine days' humbling). (S 104–106)
- The doctrine is "contained in 49 Tables. In 49 voyces, or callings: which are the Natural Keyes, to
  open those, not 49 but 48 (for One is not to be opened) Gates of understanding." (Nalvage,
  12 April 1584; T 171)
- "the Soul of man hath no portion in this first Table"; "the first Table can have no call, it is of
  the Godhead". (T 173, 192)
- "every Table hath his key: every key openeth his gate"; the first Call is "for the moving of the
  second Table"; it moves its kings and ministers "to visible apparition". (T 182)
- The Calls are "entrances into the knowledge of the mystical Tables: Every Table containing one
  whole leaf, whereunto you need no other circumstances"; "the second, with you is the first".
  (Ilemese, 11 July 1584; T 289)
- The Calls are "uttered of me, backward: and of you, in practise, forward". (T 172)

## 5. What is said of particular leaves

- Leaf 1, opening rows: "a preface of the Creation and distinction of Angels"; rows 22–23: "parcells
  of Invitations very pleasant to good Angels". (Dee, S 78)
- "the eightenth leaf which was of the spirites of the earth": Kelley read part aloud and spirits
  appeared. (15 April 1583; S 91)
- No other leaf is described, and no word of leaves 2–48 is translated anywhere in the record.

## 6. The letters of the Calls located in the tables

- 13 April 1584: each letter of the first words is given with a table and a number — "A In the 21th.
  Table. 11406 downward"; "D The 41th. from the Center ascending, and so to the right upper Angle,
  27004"; "Z The 46th ascending 312004". (T 173–174)
- "we teach places to be numbred: letters to be elected from the numbred, and proper words from the
  letters." (Gabriel, T 186)
- After a few words: "I will first go through the Letters, and after come to the Numbers" — and the
  numbers are not resumed. The rule that turns a number into a place is nowhere given. (T 174, 177)

## 7. The manuscript itself (Sloane 3189, from the images)

- **Title** on the leaf before the text: "Liber Mysteriorum, Sextus et Sanctus". A later note calls
  the hand Edward Kelley's ("Dr Dee's" struck out).
- **Leaf 1.** Only side 2 is in this manuscript (ff.3r–9r): running words in numbered paragraphs, then
  nine lines of spaced single letters. A later note says side 1 "is not (as it ought to be)
  transcribed here", its first rows having a word to a square and so being "writ thus at large".
  Side 1 as printed comes from the diary (Sloane 3188).
- **Points and accents** are on leaves 2a–5a: a full stop inside the cell at the foot of the letter;
  an acute stroke over the vowel. None was seen on 5b–7a (top rows and one lower band checked).
- **Leaves 25b–28b (ff.33v–36v).** Every square is filled and no letter stands upright. One series is
  tilted about 45° clockwise; the series on the alternate squares is turned about half round from
  it along the same diagonal. To read one series the leaf is turned one way, to read the other it
  is turned the opposite way. Pen and size are the same; no note says how to read them. The first
  rows of f.33v are mostly upright.
- **Leaves 29–48 (ff.37r–56v) are not lozenges.** They use the same square 49 × 49 ruling; tilted
  letters stand on alternate squares only and the squares between are empty. They are chessboard
  leaves with one series written. Some have blocks of upright capitals at the corners or the
  middles of the sides.
- **Note at the head of leaf 43a (f.51r):** "These letters are to be made thorowout these 6 tables
  as they are here … but once heare yt is but a note of them for every empty square is to be filled
  as these are" (middle words doubtful).
- **Unusual leaves:** 8a (f.16r) a row of capitals and an isolated word; 9a (f.17r) a ring of
  capitals and four boxed corners; 10b and 11a (ff.18v, 19r) heavy inset blocks of letters.
- **The last leaf (f.57):** empty ruled grids with two lines of angelic characters beneath (15 and
  7 on the recto). The "21 words" are not written out as such.
- **Script:** Latin letters throughout; angelic characters only as one-line titles above tables
  (f.3r, ff.10–21, f.56v) and under the empty grids of f.57.
- **Order:** no row or column is numbered; paragraph numbers on leaf 1 and the foliation run
  forwards. Nothing on the pages marks a reading from the back.
- **Latin prayers** written at f.26v ("Soli deo, laus, et honor sempiternus") and f.32v.
- **The hand:** z (long hooked tail) and x (small cross) are distinct; n and u can be told apart only
  here and there on upright pages, and not on the tilted ones.

## 9. What follows for the study

- The question "what does it say?" is answered by the record: nothing, in the sense of a discourse
  to translate. What can be studied is the form — and the form found (names, families, houses, the
  regions of the book, the roads of the chessboard and diamond leaves) is a description of the
  instrument, not a decipherment.
- Work that the record makes worth doing: the 49th table; the order and direction of the book as
  Dee meant to write it; the correspondence of Calls and tables as a matter of use; the accents and
  points as a guide to speaking leaf 1 and leaves 2–5; the names by leaf, as the catalogue gives them.
- Work that the record makes pointless: any further search for sentences or a cipher of meaning in
  the letters of the tables.
